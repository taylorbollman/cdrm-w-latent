Review findings:
- build_adamw(..., fused=None) preserves the historical optimizer default.
  Explicit fused=True retains FP32 trainable parameters, native decay groups,
  and exactly one optimizer owner for tied parameters. Conflicting foreach=True
  and fused=True choices are rejected.
- complete_update_parity accepts an optimizer_factory, uses it instead of the
  legacy builder, and records the actual optimizer flags. The full-CE batch
  factory remains active for every update in both parity arms.
- The fixed-gradient probe restores identical raw gradients before every one
  of three scalar and three fused AdamW updates. It compares moments, final
  parameters and cumulative update deltas independently.
- Clipping, LR/scheduler values, counters, ownership and optimizer group
  hyperparameters must match exactly; only the fused selector may differ.
  CPU/CUDA placement of the Adam step scalar is intentionally not an equality
  requirement, but its value is.
- A successful optimizer step increments the physical-update counter even if
  a later scheduler call fails. State restoration happens in finally, copying
  back the original weights and raw gradients without replacing their buffers.
- Tests cover restored values and addresses, tied ownership, partial failure,
  nonfinite state rejection, update errors hidden by near-identical whole
  weights, and tiny unexpected optimizer-hyperparameter changes.
- Global scalar reductions use math.fsum to avoid Python-version-dependent sum
  rounding in retained comparison evidence.

Prospective fixed-gradient budget rationale:
The moment and final-weight relative-L2 and maximum/reference-peak limits of
1e-6 are about eight FP32 machine epsilons. They allow a small amount of changed
rounding from fused arithmetic while remaining a tight implementation check
over three updates with the same fixed raw gradients. These are engineering
screens, not limits reported by the RT authors or guarantees of training quality.

Final-weight agreement alone can hide a bad optimizer update because pretrained
weights are much larger than one small update. A separate cumulative-update
relative-L2 limit of 1e-3 therefore restricts the discrepancy to 0.1% of the
reference update's L2 magnitude. That comparison uses FP64 subtraction of the
actual final and initial FP32 values. Maximum update-relative errors are only
descriptive: an almost-zero coordinate update can be dominated by one FP32
weight-spacing increment and is not a useful standalone maximum-ratio gate.
The moment/final-weight maximum gates remain enforced. All budgets were fixed
before the actual-model GPU comparison; CPU success does not waive GPU misses.
